# D8-18 — Price all three resources, and scale the frame to the machine

[Back to EPIC-08](../epics/EPIC-08-depth.md) · [Backlog index](../../BACKLOG.md)

**Outcome:** Two player reports from a mid-game screenshot showing 106 Matter beside 830 Energy and 253 Data: "the map is vast but we don't have as much resources, when building a city", and "it's already laggy".

**Owns:** costs across `src/data/buildings.ts` and `src/data/units.ts`, `AI.energyWorkerRatio`, `WORKER_CARGO_CAPACITY`, `mergeStatic` in `rendering/models/buildings.ts`, the glow-material quantisation in `palette.ts`, `RenderQuality`/`nextQuality` in `Renderer.ts`, `WorldScene.setShadowQuality`, the GPU rows in the diagnostics overlay, and `?seed=`/`?quality=`.

**Acceptance:**
- Every resource is spent: defence and production lean on Energy, the Generation III roster leans on Data, and a synthesis plant is bought mainly with the resource it converts *from*, so a Matter-starved colony can still afford the thing that makes Matter.
- A Worker carries 14 rather than 10, so a haul from an expansion on a 300x224 map is worth making.
- The opponent's Worker split matches the new cost mix and is derived from it rather than guessed.
- A structure costs a handful of draw calls instead of thirty-odd, with no visible change to any model.
- The renderer scales itself: sustained poor frame rate steps resolution down, then bloom, then shadow resolution, and it climbs back only after a long comfortable stretch.
- `?seed=` pins a match and `?quality=` pins a tier, so a report can be reproduced on another machine.
- The diagnostics overlay reports draw calls, triangles, rendered megapixels, and the current quality tier.

**Verify:** `Renderer.test.ts` covers the quality hysteresis; the full suite covers the cost changes through every gate that spends resources; measured draw calls, megapixels, income, and survival times recorded below.

## Outcome

Supply was never the problem: a seeded map holds about 7800 Matter per faction and a ten-structure walled city costs about 1200. Demand was. Almost everything was priced in Matter alone, so Matter was the only wall a builder ever hit while the other two resources piled up unspent — exactly the screenshot that was reported.

Decision: spread the prices instead of enriching the map. Enriching deposits would have made the safe home cluster better and the contested middle pointless; repricing makes the resources the player already has worth having. Worker cargo went 10 → 14 in the same pass, which scales income with *distance* rather than with node richness — the honest answer to a vast map.

Decision: the opponent's Worker split is derived. A Worker returns Matter about 1.5x faster than Energy and the roster now spends roughly 60:40, so about half the crew belongs on Energy; left at a quarter the opponent banked Matter it could not spend and stalled at 20 Energy with the Data for a Generation it could not buy. Two rounds of tuning by feel made it worse before the arithmetic made it right.

Decision: merge each structure's static meshes. Measured, a structure cost 34 draw calls and a 23-building colony 1281; the pieces never move relative to each other, so everything except the animated parts is baked into one mesh per material and cached per kind and team. The same colony now costs 917, and a player's city is where that compounds.

Decision: quality follows the frame rate, not a settings screen. A 2000px window on a Retina panel asks for 8.0 megapixels a frame through a bloom pass; the medium tier is 4.5 and the low tier 2.0 with bloom off. Stepping down takes three seconds of trouble and stepping back up twenty-five of comfort, because a symmetric rule restores exactly the load that caused the step down and an oscillating resolution is worse to look at than a steady lower one.

Measured after the rebalance, with a colony that builds economy and defences and never produces a combat Agent: fortified, it survived the full forty minutes on two of three seeds and held its first damage off to 741s on the third; unfortified it still lost at 749-892s.
