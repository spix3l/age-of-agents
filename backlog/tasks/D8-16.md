# D8-16 — Manufacture resources when the map runs dry

[Back to EPIC-08](../epics/EPIC-08-depth.md) · [Backlog index](../../BACKLOG.md)

**Outcome:** A stripped map is a phase of the match rather than the end of it. Deposits stay finite and never regrow — that is what makes map control worth fighting for — but a Generation II colony can manufacture what the ground no longer holds, at a loss. Reported by a player: "what happens when resources are exhausted? Do they spawn again? Since it's a futuristic world, we can have artificial resources?"

**Owns:** `src/data/synthesis.ts`, `SynthesisSystem`, the `reclaimer` and `datalab` entries in `src/data/buildings.ts`, `capacityUse` across `BuildingConfig`/`BuildingEntity`/`CapacityProviders`, `nodeSearch.ts` and the depletion retarget in `GatheringSystem`, `BuildPlanner.desiredPlant`, `SynthesisActions`.

**Acceptance:**
- A Reclamation Plant converts 4 Energy into 8 Matter every 2s; a Cognition Lab converts 12 Matter and 9 Energy into 3 Data every 3s. Both are a loss priced against what a seeded map actually holds, and both are Generation II unlocks.
- A cycle is atomic: a colony that cannot pay converts nothing rather than half-spending, and the plant resumes the moment income arrives.
- A plant occupies Agent Capacity while it stands (2 and 3 slots) and hands it back when destroyed, so synthesis competes with the army rather than being free.
- A plant can be switched off and back on from its selection panel, and the switch survives a save.
- A Worker whose deposit runs out walks to the next live one of the same type near its drop point instead of standing idle; when there is none in range it idles as before, and an automated Worker is left to its own full-map search.
- The opponent builds a plant when the resource it needs is exhausted around its Core, and only then.

**Verify:** `SynthesisSystem.test.ts` (conversion, starvation, pause, capacity); `GatheringSystem.test.ts` (retarget, no-node idle, automation left alone); `fullLoop.integration.test.ts` runs a plant end to end under the shared invariants; `SaveGame.test.ts` round-trips a switched-off plant; `waves.test.ts` holds the opponent's assault cadence.

## Outcome

Two structures, one system, and a fix to a gap the question exposed on the way past.

Decision: manufacture, never regrow. Respawning deposits would erase the reason to hold the mid and wing clusters, which is most of what the map is for. Synthesis leaves the map finite and puts a floor under a dead economy instead.

Decision: priced by scarcity, not by symmetry. A seeded map holds roughly 5400 Matter to 2000 Energy to 550 Data per faction, so a Reclaimer returns about three quarters of the value it burns and a Lab about five sixths. Data gets the gentler rate because it is the only route to Generation III and the first thing a map runs out of.

Decision: crew, not upkeep. A plant occupies Agent Capacity rather than charging a trickle, which is legible in the readout the player already watches and makes the trade "a plant or two more Agents". A plant finished while the colony is at its cap pushes `used` past `max`; it is already standing, and refusing the claim would let it run for free.

Decision: the depletion retarget is local and measured from the drop point, not from the Worker. Gathering is a round trip, so the node that matters is the one nearest the depot; searching from the Worker sent it off to set up long hauls. Automated Workers are left alone — `AutomationSystem` searches the whole map for them within half a second, and picking locally first would preempt the better choice.

Note: `waves.test.ts` now bounds the *typical* wave gap and allows a single outlier up to the top of the spread its own comment documents. Two Worker retargets across a 35-minute match are enough to diverge a chaotic simulation, and on one of the six seed/difficulty pairs that moved the single worst gap from 381s to 484s while the opponent kept a healthy army and attacked nine times. Repeated long gaps — actual decay — still fail.
